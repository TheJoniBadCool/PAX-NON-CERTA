    PaxNonCerta Rules

    1. Board Size and Player Corners

    Choose a 10×10, 12×12, 14×14, or 16×16 board before starting. Players spawn in the 4 corners: Red (Top-Left), Blue (Top-Right), Green (Bottom-Right), Yellow (Bottom-Left).

    2. Movement Rules & Unmovable Towers

    • Movable pieces (Pawn, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King) move exactly 1 square in any direction.
    • Pawns move on odd-numbered rounds only (Rounds 1, 3, 5…). On even-numbered rounds, pawns cannot initiate movement, merging, unmerging, transfers, or attacks, but remain valid attack targets and may receive non-Pawn units. A ready siege assault and a Sally Out are exceptions.
    • A stack containing any Pawn can move as a whole only on odd-numbered rounds. On even-numbered rounds, non-Pawn units in that stack may still be unmerged, moved, or transferred individually; the stack may receive non-Pawn units and remains a valid attack target. New sieges may be deployed only on odd-numbered rounds.
    • Towers (Rooks) are UNMOVABLE: Each tower has one randomly chosen gate, marked by an arrow pointing north, south, east, or west. Merging, transfers, unmerging, and sieges can only enter or exit through that adjacent square. Towers start with at least two squares between them in every direction, including diagonals.

    3. Multi-Piece Turns

    During your turn, you can move 0, 1, or MULTIPLE of your movable surviving pieces at once!

    Terrain

    Each game generates roughly 50% plains, 20% forests (🌲), 20% hills (⛰) and 10% mountains (▲), rounded to whole squares. Castles always start on plains and mountain squares cannot be entered or used for retreat re-entry. Starting units and castle gates are never blocked by mountains. Forest forces are hidden from opponents and gain +25% strength when they remain there and are attacked. A force that held a hill for the entire previous round gains +25% strength when it stays and is attacked. Arriving forces and opposing swaps receive no terrain defence. Bonuses apply before the odds curve; plains have no effect. Entering an unseen enemy's square resolves normally, including the usual rules for prisoners. Concealment is visual; multiplayer guests receive the board data and the host can inspect it.

    4. Same-Player Merging

    Move onto one of your own pieces to choose Merge or Cancel. Accepted merges commit the involved pieces for the turn, and multiple pieces can merge into one stack. If two or more same-player pieces plan the same destination, they merge automatically. Towers take priority; otherwise the result follows Tower, Pawn, Horse, Bishop, King, Queen precedence. Same-player paths never intercept each other. Click or tap a piece to select the whole stack. Right-click or long-press it to open its unit list; clicking a catapult or besieged Tower also opens the list and its siege action. Select a movable unit, then click or tap an adjacent square to unmerge it. Choosing an adjacent same-player piece prompts for transfer confirmation.

    5. Simultaneous Resolution & Collisions

    All players submit their secret plans. All moves happen at the exact same instant.
    Resolution follows this order: planned prisoner releases and executions are announced and applied first, pieces leave their original squares, opposing swaps are resolved, same-player pieces merge on each planned square, then every remaining opposing stack on that square fights.
    • Target Collision: 2+ pieces move into the same square.
    • Swap Interception: Piece A moves to S2 while Piece B moves to S1 (crossing paths).
    • Stationary Collision: Piece A moves into Piece B which stayed stationary.
    Same-player candidates at a destination become one stack before fighting. All opposing stacks then fight using weighted odds: counted units provide stack strength, a side receives a 20% bonus for each counted unit it has above the strongest opposing stack, and the final strength is curved to make larger stacks more influential without removing randomness. King, Queen, Bishop morale, experience, armour, and stationary Tower bonuses are also shown on the battle cards. A defeated Queen or King fighting alone has a base capture risk of 60% or 75%, respectively, before the usual battle modifiers; prisoners do not count as escorts. Defeated casualties are captured instead of dying: the winning player chooses Prisoner or Execute for each captured unit before proceeding. Winner casualties still die. Other surviving losers retreat for 3 rounds. Every retreat adds one Routed stack to each fleeing unit (maximum 3): -20% battle strength and +25 percentage points casualty/capture risk per stack. Routed lasts for the re-entry round and the next five rounds, then expires. Merging, splitting, and transferring do not reset penalties.
    Prisoners: Keep their original artwork and color, with chains in their captor's color. They belong to the captor, move/unmerge/transfer only on odd rounds, and can merge into friendly stacks, including Towers through their gates. Prisoner-only stacks cannot attack, always lose when attacked, and all their units are captured, never routed or killed by battle. In mixed stacks they provide no battle bonuses, suffer no casualties, and follow the winner's custody. Recapturing your original units restores them to normal service. Conflicting moves between prisoner-only stacks are cancelled if neither side can fight.
    Click a prisoner-containing piece to open its unit list, then use Execute (skull) or Release (white flag) to queue a private decision. Reset Plans cancels it. Existing prisoner releases and executions are announced in the combat log and resolved first, before movement. The remaining stack may also move that round; the scheduled prisoner cannot also unmerge or transfer. Release returns units to their original player through the 3-round re-entry system without routed penalties; if no square is free, they wait rather than die. Released units of defeated players remain stationary. Prisoners count as alive for objectives until executed. Newly captured units still receive the winner's fate choice during battle resolution.

    6. Sieges

    A Tower attack requires at least one attacking non-prisoner Pawn and starts a siege instead of an immediate battle; new sieges may be deployed only on odd-numbered rounds. The attacker remains on its source square as a catapult, with an assault timer of 3 rounds; when it reaches 0, it may launch at the adjacent Tower even on an even-numbered round. The besieged Tower displays a 6-round timer and crossed marks. After the Tower timer reaches 0, it loses one random non-prisoner, non-Tower unit each round. Defenders cannot move, transfer, merge, or unmerge units out of the Tower square while it is under siege. Defending non-Tower units may instead choose Sally Out if at least one can fight, including on even rounds. Defeated casualties are captured; surviving defenders return inside the Tower, and the siege continues if the catapult survives. Counterattacks exclude the Tower. Tower conquest remains automatic; non-Tower siege defenders are captured instead of forcibly killed.

    7. Objectives and Victory

    Your private checklist awards +4 for a target enemy's defeat, +3 for a target enemy King's death, +2 for a target enemy Queen's death, and +1 for controlling a specific enemy castle. Targets are randomly chosen at match start. Lose 4 points if your King dies and 3 for each starting castle you lose. Castle points reverse when ownership changes back; retreating units are still alive. Scores are checked after the whole round, including siege casualties and retreat re-entry. Players reaching -10 points are permanently defeated together, then enemy-defeat rewards are awarded. Reach 10 points to win; simultaneous winners share victory. Losing your King alone does not eliminate you, and being the last player does not automatically win. If everyone is defeated, the match is a draw.
