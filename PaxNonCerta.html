PaxNonCerta Rules

1. 16x16 Quadrupled Board

4 players spawn in each of the 4 corners: Red (Top-Left), Blue (Top-Right), Green (Bottom-Right), Yellow (Bottom-Left).

2. Movement Rules & Unmovable Towers

• Movable pieces (Pawn, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King) move exactly 1 square in any direction.
• Pawns move on odd-numbered rounds only (Rounds 1, 3, 5…). On even-numbered rounds, pawns cannot initiate movement, merging, unmerging, transfers, or attacks, but remain valid attack targets and may receive non-Pawn units. A ready siege assault and a Sally Out are exceptions.
• A stack containing any Pawn can move as a whole only on odd-numbered rounds. On even-numbered rounds, non-Pawn units in that stack may still be unmerged, moved, or transferred individually; the stack may receive non-Pawn units and remains a valid attack target. New sieges may be deployed only on odd-numbered rounds.
• Towers (Rooks) are UNMOVABLE: Each tower has one randomly chosen gate, marked by an arrow pointing north, south, east, or west. Merging, transfers, unmerging, and sieges can only enter or exit through that adjacent square. Towers start with at least two squares between them in every direction, including diagonals.

3. Multi-Piece Turns

During your turn, you can move 0, 1, or MULTIPLE of your movable surviving pieces at once!

4. Same-Player Merging

Move onto one of your own pieces to choose Merge or Cancel. Accepted merges commit the involved pieces for the turn, and multiple pieces can merge into one stack. If two or more same-player pieces plan the same destination, they merge automatically. Towers take priority; otherwise the result follows Tower, Pawn, Horse, Bishop, King, Queen precedence. Same-player paths never intercept each other. Click or tap a piece to select the whole stack. Right-click or long-press it to open its unit list; clicking a catapult or besieged Tower also opens the list and its siege action. Select a movable unit, then click or tap an adjacent square to unmerge it. Choosing an adjacent same-player piece prompts for transfer confirmation.

5. Simultaneous Resolution & Collisions

All players submit their secret plans. All moves happen at the exact same instant.
Resolution follows this order: pieces leave their original squares, opposing swaps are resolved first, same-player pieces merge on each planned square, then every remaining opposing stack on that square fights.
• Target Collision: 2+ pieces move into the same square.
• Swap Interception: Piece A moves to S2 while Piece B moves to S1 (crossing paths).
• Stationary Collision: Piece A moves into Piece B which stayed stationary.
Same-player candidates at a destination become one stack before fighting. All opposing stacks then fight using weighted odds: counted units provide stack strength, a side receives a 20% bonus for each counted unit it has above the strongest opposing stack, and the final strength is curved to make larger stacks more influential without removing randomness. King, Queen, Bishop morale, experience, armour, and stationary Tower bonuses are also shown on the battle cards.

6. Sieges

A Tower attack requires at least one attacking Pawn and starts a siege instead of an immediate battle; new sieges may be deployed only on odd-numbered rounds. The attacker remains on its source square as a catapult, with an assault timer of 3 rounds; when it reaches 0, it may launch at the adjacent Tower even on an even-numbered round. The besieged Tower displays a 6-round timer and crossed marks. After the Tower timer reaches 0, it loses one random non-Tower unit each round. Defenders cannot move, transfer, merge, or unmerge units out of the Tower square while it is under siege. The full defending non-Tower stack may instead choose Sally Out at any time, including on an even-numbered round, to battle the catapult; casualties disappear, surviving defenders return inside the Tower, and the siege continues if the catapult survives. Counterattacks exclude the Tower.

7. King Elimination

Capturing a player's King eliminates that player. Last remaining player wins!
